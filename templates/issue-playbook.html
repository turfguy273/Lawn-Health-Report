What we say for each common issue

When a technician picks one of these issues in Service Autopilot, this is the writeup that gets pulled into the customer's report. Read through each one and mark anything you'd word differently — once approved, this becomes the fixed language the automation uses every time.

01

Too Much Irrigation

What it looks like

Spongy, soggy turf, standing water after watering, mushrooms popping up, and grass that stays wet long after it should have dried out.

What this means

When grass gets more water than it needs, the roots sit in soggy soil and can't get enough oxygen, so they weaken and grow shallow instead of deep. It also keeps the lawn wet for long stretches — exactly the kind of damp, humid conditions fungus needs to take hold.

What we'll do

We'll recommend a shorter, less frequent watering schedule and check for irrigation heads that may be stuck on or misaligned. If the soil's compacted and draining poorly, we may recommend aeration to help water move through instead of pooling.

02

Not Enough Irrigation

What it looks like

Grass takes on a dull, grayish-blue tint before browning, footprints or mower tracks stay visible for a long time, and blades feel dry and brittle.

What this means

Without enough water, grass can't keep its cells full and firm, so it starts shutting down to conserve moisture — that's the color change you're seeing. If it continues, the grass will brown and go dormant to survive.

What we'll do

We'll recommend a deep, infrequent watering schedule (better than frequent light watering) and may suggest aeration so water soaks in instead of running off, especially on slopes and compacted areas.

03

Shade

What it looks like

Thin, sparse grass under trees or near structures, slower growth than the rest of the yard, and sometimes moss moving in.

What this means

Grass needs a good amount of direct sunlight to fuel its own growth. In heavy shade it thins out naturally, and thinner grass is also more vulnerable to wear and disease — even with everything else done right.

What we'll do

We'll recommend selective trimming of overhanging branches to open up more light, use a shade-tolerant seed blend when we overseed this area, and may suggest raising the mowing height here specifically. Some thinning is normal in heavy shade even with treatment.

04

Crepe Myrtle Bark Scale

What it looks like

A white or gray crusty coating on the branches and trunk, often with a black, sooty-looking residue on the bark and leaves below it.

What this means

This is a small scale insect that attaches to the bark and feeds on the tree. As it feeds, it excretes a sugary residue that grows black sooty mold — that's the dark coating you're seeing. It's unsightly and stresses the tree, though it's rarely fatal on its own.

What we'll do

We'll apply a treatment targeted at the scale insects and, for heavy buildup, may recommend a soft scrub of the trunk to remove the crust. We'll keep an eye on the tree's recovery on future visits.

05

Whitefly

What it looks like

Tiny white insects that flutter up when a shrub is disturbed, yellowing or speckled leaves, and a sticky residue with dark mold on the foliage.

What this means

Whiteflies feed on the underside of leaves and drain the plant's sap, which weakens it over time. Like the scale insect, they leave behind a sugary residue that grows the same sooty black mold.

What we'll do

We'll apply a treatment labeled for whitefly control on the affected shrubs. Whitefly populations can bounce back, so we'll plan a follow-up treatment and monitor on our next visit.

06

Pythium Blight

What it looks like

Irregular brown, greasy-looking patches that can appear almost overnight, often starting in low spots or slopes where water collects.

What this means

Pythium is a fungus that thrives when grass blades stay wet for long stretches, especially in hot, humid weather. It spreads fast along the path water travels, which is why it often shows up first on slopes and low areas.

What we'll do

We'll apply a fungicide labeled for Pythium control, recommend watering only in the early morning so blades dry out during the day, and suggest avoiding mowing the area while it's wet to keep it from spreading. We'll overseed once it's under control.

07

Mowed Too Short (Scalping)

What it looks like

Brown or yellow stressed grass right after a mow, stems and crowns visibly exposed, and sometimes weeds moving into the thinned area.

What this means

Cutting grass too short removes more of the leaf blade than the plant can afford, which cuts into its ability to photosynthesize and shocks the root system. It also opens up bare soil where weeds are quick to move in.

What we'll do

We'll recommend raising the mowing height to what's right for this grass type going forward, and overseed any bare patches that developed. The lawn should bounce back on its own once it's cut at the correct height.
