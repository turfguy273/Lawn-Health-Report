What we found

Irregular brown, thinning patches on the slope

There are a couple of connected tan-brown patches on the upper part of your back slope, right along the fence. The grass in the middle of these spots is thin and dry, while the lawn around it is still green and healthy — so this is a localized problem, not something affecting the whole yard.

Our leading suspicion is Pythium Blight, a fungal disease that hits Bermudagrass hard in exactly this kind of weather. This year's pattern of hard heat and drought, followed by sudden afternoon storms and heavy overnight humidity, is close to a textbook setup for it to take hold.

What this means for you

Weather stress opened the door for disease

The drought and heat this summer already weakened the grass in this spot. Then the recent evening showers and humidity kept the grass blades wet all night long — and that prolonged leaf wetness is exactly what Pythium needs to infect and spread. Slopes and low-lying areas like yours tend to hold moisture and show it first, and Pythium is notorious for spreading fast along water flow.

This isn't a sign of poor lawn care — it's a weather-driven disease outbreak, and it's treatable. Left unchecked, though, Pythium can spread quickly overnight, especially if humid, wet conditions continue.

What we'll do about it

Our plan for this spot

  • Confirm Pythium Blight with an on-site inspection (greasy, matted blades and a distinct smell are tell-tale signs).
  • Apply a fungicide labeled for Pythium control on Bermudagrass.
  • Recommend watering only in the early morning, never in the evening, so blades dry out during the day instead of staying wet overnight.
  • Advise holding off on mowing this area while it's wet to avoid spreading spores to healthy turf.
  • Overseed the damaged patches once the fungus is controlled so they fill back in.
  • Re-check this spot on our next visit and note progress in your file.
