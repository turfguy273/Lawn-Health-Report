Weed ID Guide

Common Lawn Weed

Dallisgrass

Paspalum dilatatum

A coarse, fast-growing clumping grass that shows up in almost every lawn type across the region — and one of the most stubborn weeds to fully clear once it's established.

Growth habit
Circular clumps
Blade color
Pale yellow-green
Texture
Coarse, wide blades
Seed head
Finger-like spikes
How to spot it

The telltale signs

  • Clumps, not patches — Dallisgrass grows outward from a single point, forming a circular clump rather than spreading evenly like turfgrass.
  • Wider, paler blades — even at a glance, the blade width and lighter color stand out against finer, darker turfgrass.
  • Tall seed stalks within days of mowing — it shoots up narrow seed stalks with 3–8 finger-like spikes (often tinged purple-black from a common fungus) noticeably faster than the surrounding lawn.
  • Comes back in the same spot — it spreads by both short rhizomes and heavy reseeding, so clumps often reappear in the same area year after year if not fully treated.

In Fescue lawns

Fescue is already a coarser, clump-forming grass, so Dallisgrass can blend in more from a distance — but its faster vertical growth between mowings and slightly yellower tone still give it away.

Why it's a problem

More than just an eyesore

Dallisgrass competes aggressively with turfgrass for water, light, and nutrients, and a single mature clump can produce thousands of seeds in a season. Mowing over seed heads can spread it to new areas of the yard, and its deep, tough root system makes it far more resilient than most lawn weeds — one treatment rarely clears it completely.

Our approach

How Advanced Turf Care treats it

  • Spot-treat individual clumps with a selective herbicide labeled for Dallisgrass and safe for your specific grass type.
  • Plan for repeat treatments through the season, since established clumps regrow from rhizomes and buried seed.
  • Avoid mowing over seed heads where possible to limit spread to untreated areas.
  • Follow up on prior clumps at future visits to catch regrowth early, before it reseeds again.
